# Academic Writing Guide

## Core Principles
- **Precision** — every claim should be supported and specific
- **Objectivity** — acknowledge limitations and counter-arguments
- **Rigor** — methodology and evidence matter more than opinion
- **Contribution** — what does this add to existing knowledge?

## Academic Paper Structure (IMRaD)

### Abstract
- Summarize the entire paper in 150-300 words
- Include: purpose, method, key findings, conclusion
- Write last (after you know what you're summarizing)

### Introduction
- Establish the problem and why it matters
- Review relevant literature (what's known, what's missing)
- State your research question or thesis
- Preview your approach
- **Funnel structure:** Broad context → specific problem → your contribution

### Methods
- Detailed enough to replicate
- Justify your choices
- Address limitations honestly
- Past tense ("participants completed...")

### Results
- Present findings without interpretation
- Use tables and figures for complex data
- Report statistical significance where appropriate
- Don't cherry-pick — include null results

### Discussion
- Interpret results in context of existing literature
- Address how findings answer your research question
- Acknowledge limitations
- Suggest future research directions
- Don't overclaim

### Conclusion
- Brief restatement of key findings and significance
- Practical implications
- No new information

## Academic Style

### Do
- Use precise, discipline-specific terminology
- Cite sources for all claims that aren't original
- Define key terms early
- Use hedging language appropriately ("suggests," "indicates," "may")
- Maintain consistent formatting (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.)

### Don't
- Use first person casually (some disciplines allow "I/we" for methods; check conventions)
- Make unsupported claims
- Use informal language or contractions
- Assume the reader knows your specific context
- Plagiarize (obviously) — paraphrase and cite

## Citation Basics

| Style | Common In | In-Text Example |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| APA | Social sciences, psychology | (Smith, 2024) |
| MLA | Humanities, literature | (Smith 42) |
| Chicago | History, some humanities | Footnotes or (Smith 2024, 42) |
| IEEE | Engineering, CS | [1] |

**Golden rule:** When in doubt, cite it. Over-citation is better than under-citation.

## Common Academic Writing Mistakes
1. **Vague thesis** — "This paper explores..." (What do you argue?)
2. **Literature dump** — listing sources without synthesizing them
3. **Overclaiming** — "This proves..." (very little in research is "proven")
4. **Ignoring counter-arguments** — weakens your position
5. **Passive voice overuse** — some passive is fine; all passive is unreadable
