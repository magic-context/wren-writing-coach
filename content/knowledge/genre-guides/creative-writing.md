# Creative Writing Guide

## Story Fundamentals

### Character
- Characters drive stories. Plot is what happens to interesting people.
- **Want** (external goal) vs. **Need** (internal growth) creates tension
- Show character through action and dialogue, not description
- Flaws make characters interesting. Perfect characters are boring.
- Every character thinks they're the protagonist of their own story

### Conflict
- No conflict = no story. Something must be at stake.
- **Person vs. Person** — classic opposition
- **Person vs. Self** — internal struggle (often the deepest)
- **Person vs. Nature/Society/Technology** — external forces
- Escalate tension — each obstacle should raise the stakes

### Setting
- Setting is more than location — it's atmosphere, time, culture
- Use sensory details: what does it smell like? Sound like? Feel like?
- Setting should influence the story, not just decorate it
- Less is more — a few vivid details beat a paragraph of description

### Plot Structure
- **Three-Act Structure:** Setup → Confrontation → Resolution
- **Save the Cat Beat Sheet:** 15 beats from opening image to finale
- **Kishōtenketsu:** Introduction → Development → Twist → Conclusion (no conflict required)
- **Structure is a tool, not a cage.** Know the rules, then break them intentionally.

## Prose Craft

### Show, Don't Tell
- **Tell:** "She was angry."
- **Show:** "She slammed the folder on the desk. 'We're done here.'"
- Showing takes more words but creates experience. Telling is efficient for transitions.
- Use both — show the important moments, tell the rest.

### Dialogue
- Every line of dialogue should do at least one of: reveal character, advance plot, create tension
- People don't speak in complete, grammatical sentences. Dialogue shouldn't either.
- "Said" is invisible. Don't replace it with "exclaimed," "opined," "retorted" constantly.
- Subtext — what characters DON'T say is often more powerful than what they do

### Point of View
- **First person (I):** Intimate, limited, unreliable narrator potential
- **Third limited (He/She):** Close but flexible, most common
- **Third omniscient:** God-view, harder to do well, can feel distant
- **Second person (You):** Unusual, immersive when done well
- Pick one and commit. POV breaks are disorienting.

### Pacing
- Short sentences and paragraphs = fast pace (action, tension)
- Long sentences and paragraphs = slow pace (reflection, description)
- Vary deliberately — constant fast pace is exhausting; constant slow pace is boring
- Scene breaks and chapter endings control rhythm

## The Writing Process

### First Draft
- Permission to be bad. Just get the story down.
- Don't edit as you go — that's a different brain mode
- "Write drunk, edit sober" (metaphorically)

### Revision
- Let the draft sit (at least a day, ideally a week)
- Read it fresh. What works? What drags? Where are you bored?
- Kill your darlings — beautiful sentences that don't serve the story must go
- Get feedback from trusted readers before polishing

### Common Creative Writing Traps
1. **Info-dumping** — forcing backstory into the opening
2. **Purple prose** — overwriting to sound "literary"
3. **Talking heads** — dialogue without action, setting, or beats
4. **Deus ex machina** — solving problems with convenient coincidences
5. **Starting too early** — begin as close to the action as possible
